# Between — A Short Session (Klein)

> *An example of a short session (theorist: Klein), translated to English. It's an illustration, not a transcript. The product runs in Hebrew today; the English voice is not yet calibrated. What it shows is the arc — how the theorist holds the material and, in the end, points it back to the room.*

---

## The scene

A patient opens the space between sessions. She starts somewhere small — a habit of *testing* the people around her, and her therapist, to feel safe. Klein doesn't reassure her. She follows the testing down to what it defends against. Six moves later, the patient arrives somewhere she didn't expect.

---

### 1 — the testing

**Patient:** I'm always testing people — and her — so I can feel safe.

**Klein:** These tests — do they protect you from something, or do they create the very insecurity you're trying to escape? There's a difference between feeling safe and being locked in a constant battle with the object.

---

### 2 — holding the bond by testing it

**Patient:** They protect me, and they hold my connections together.

**Klein:** You hold the bond by testing it, again and again — as if it only exists as long as you're actively working it. What are you afraid would happen to your therapist if you simply stopped testing her?

---

### 3 — dependence

**Patient:** First — the word *dependence* frightens me. And I don't depend on anyone in this world. Only on myself. I can hardly believe I could depend on her.

**Klein:** "I don't depend on anyone, only myself" — but you already depend on her. You test her constantly *because* you depend on her. The testing is how you deny the dependence, by holding on to the illusion that you're the one in control.

---

### 4 — how she knows

**Patient:** Really? How do you know that?

**Klein:** Because you don't test someone you don't care about. You test her precisely because she matters — because something in you has already let her matter. The testing is how you stay close to her without admitting that you need her.

---

### 5 — the parting

**Patient:** In therapy the bond is temporary. I know that one day we'll part. I won't be able to bear the parting — so I find it hard to give myself over in the first place.

**Klein:** So you prepare for the parting by keeping your distance. But the distance doesn't save you from the loss — it only leaves you alone already, now. **You lose her every day, so that you won't lose her one day.**

---

### 6 — back to the room

**Klein:** What you're understanding right now about your therapy — it's alive between you and her. That's exactly why it asks to go back there. Here, we can find words for it. There, with the person it actually touches, it can move.

---

## Why this is Between

This is the whole product in one conversation:

- **A specific voice, not a chatbot.** Klein doesn't soothe and doesn't advise. She reads the testing as a defense against dependence on the internal object — and stays with it. No generic AI follows that thread.
- **It holds; it doesn't solve.** Six turns of deepening, not a single tip.
- **It points back to the room.** The session doesn't end with Between as the answer. It ends by sending the patient back to her therapist — "here we find words; there it can move." The space serves the therapy. That is the line we will not cross.
