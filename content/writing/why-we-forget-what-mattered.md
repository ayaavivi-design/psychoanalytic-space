# Why we forget what mattered most

*February 2026 · Between*

---

You leave the session knowing something.

Not in a dramatic way — not "now I understand everything." More like: a piece moved. Something that was facing one direction is now facing another. You can feel it.

Then ordinary life reassembles around you.

The session ends at 7pm. By 8pm you're making dinner. By 9pm someone needs something from you. By 10pm you're tired. The next morning you wake up inside the regular world, where what happened yesterday in that room is still technically accessible — you can describe it, if pressed — but it no longer has the same weight.

By the end of the week, you're not sure it happened at all.

---

There's a concept in psychoanalysis called *the work of mourning*. Freud used it to describe grief — the slow, repetitive process of withdrawing emotional investment from what's been lost, piece by piece, memory by memory, until the loss becomes livable.

Therapeutic insight works similarly. You don't understand something once. You understand it, then forget it, then understand it again — slightly differently, from a new angle — and forget it again, and return to it, until eventually it stops needing to be remembered because it's simply true.

This is called *working through*. It's not a sign that therapy isn't working. It's what working looks like.

---

The problem isn't the forgetting. The problem is what we do in the space where the insight used to be.

When the understanding has dissolved and we haven't yet returned to it, we're in a vulnerable interval. The old patterns are right there — they never went anywhere. And without the memory of why we were questioning them, they reassert themselves quietly, naturally, like a well-worn path.

The therapeutic hour is a disruption. Everything between sessions is the return of the ordinary.

---

This is why the space between sessions matters more than it's usually acknowledged.

Therapists know this. Most patients don't — not really, not in a way that changes how they spend Tuesday.

The question is: what do you do with Tuesday?

Some people journal. Some people do nothing, which is also a choice. Some people call a friend and talk around the thing without quite landing on it. Some people work, exercise, distract — and all of that is fine, all of that is life — but none of it holds the thread back to the room.

---

AI enters here — not to extend the session, not to replace it, but to offer a specific quality of attention during the interval.

Not the attention of a friend, who has their own life pressing in. Not the attention of a journal, which can only record what you already know you think.

The attention of a particular way of listening. Freud's ear for what the patient keeps circling but never enters. Winnicott's capacity to wait without filling the space. Bion's tolerance for sitting inside confusion until something crystallizes.

These are not therapeutic tools deployed therapeutically. They're orientations. Ways of being with your own material when the material is still loose, still circulating, not yet ready to be interpreted.

---

What tends to get lost between sessions is not the content of the session. You can usually retrieve that, with effort.

What gets lost is the *quality of attention* you were holding in the room.

The session is where you were, for fifty minutes, with someone who was entirely focused on what was alive in you. That quality of attention — that particular being-with — doesn't persist naturally. The world isn't structured for it.

Between is an attempt to make that quality of attention available at 11pm on a Thursday. Not the therapist. Not the session. Just the possibility of staying with your own material a little longer, in a way that doesn't drift.

---

The forgetting will still happen. That's not the enemy.

The enemy is the interval where nothing holds the thread, and the old patterns fill the gap without anyone noticing.

*Between is for the interval.*
